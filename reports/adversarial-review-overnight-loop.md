## 1. Findings (Most Severe First)

Our audit surfaced eight distinct issues.

### F1. Scalar "prior shift" (Hypothesis 1) cannot change human false-positive rates; differential genre shift, unpaired memo promotion, and multi-mirror boundary push did

Adding AI text faster than human text does shift the unweighted logistic intercept upward; however, uniform scalar log-odds shifts are **100% absorbed** by [`FitCutoffs`](../internal/train/train.go#L185-L203) in all pools simultaneously (even when `memo` dominates the calibration tail and `nontech-medium` sits outside calibration). Human false positives rose on Night 1 and Night 2 since **different genres shifted by different amounts** within one pooled `prose` model as unpaired memo promotions pulled the pooled cutoff *down*.

1. Order-statistic invariance of scalar intercept shift (Code + Math): suppose candidate logits shift by a constant $z'(x) = z(x) + \Delta_0$ for each text $x$. Since $\sigma(z + \Delta_0)$ increases monotonically on $\mathbb{R}$, the rank order of all human documents in the union of all pools is preserved; when raw cutoff $p^* = \sigma(z^*)$ put at most $2\%$ of `calib` (`blog + memo + essay`) above $z^*$, $p'^* = \sigma(z^* + \Delta_0)$ puts the identical `calib` subset above $z^* + \Delta_0$ **plus** puts the identical `fresh-blog` and `nontech-medium` documents above $p'^*$ despite `memo` contributing ~85% of the top-2% tail and `nontech-medium` being excluded from `docPools`. Running the repository's actual [`ngram.Train`](../internal/detect/ngram/ngram.go#L97-L166) / [`calibrate.Fit`](../internal/calibrate/calibrate.go#L150-L175) / [`FitCutoffs`](../internal/train/train.go#L185-L203) pipeline with a pure $+\ln(2.0/1.3) = +0.43$ bias shift raises `prose.LikelyAI` by `7` to `10` raw points (`50 -> 60` on production's scale) and changes `fresh-blog` and `nontech-medium` false positives by **`0` documents** (`0.00%`, 95% quantization CI `[-0.15%, +0.15%]`).
2. Observed cutoff shift was half of scalar prediction (`[Trusts brief numbers]`): on Night 2, `prose.LikelyAI` moved only `50 -> 56` ($+0.241$ logit), whereas `nontech-medium` mean raw scores rose `9.0 -> 13.4` ($+0.45$ logit) and `fresh-blog` rose `5.4 -> 7.5` ($+0.35$ logit) as several memos moved *down*. Within the code, 3 concrete mechanisms create that divergence:
   - Uneven genre prior and vocabulary drift within one n-gram table: [`TrainDirs(KindProse)`](../internal/overnight/data.go#L62-L70) pools `blog`, `memo`, `essay` plus `techblog` into one 40,000-term model (`prose.ngram.json` holds `39,795` non-zero weights). AI-to-human ratios grew unevenly (`essay` $+0.61$ log-ratio, `blog` $+0.40$, `memo` $+0.32$, `techblog` $+0.30$ **`[Trusts brief numbers]`**). `ngram.Train` learns genre-correlated unigrams and bigrams as distinct implicit intercepts per genre. Since `fresh-memo` (`32/539 = 5.94%`) supplies ~85% of all false positives in [`docPools`](../internal/overnight/metrics.go#L276), `prose.LikelyAI` tracks `memo` ($+0.24$ logit) and leaves `blog` and `nontech-medium` under-compensated by ~4 raw points.
   - Unpaired hard-negative memo promotion (`[Trusts brief numbers]`): [`mineHuman`](../internal/overnight/loop.go#L307-L356) promotes high-scoring Enron memos (`y=0`) with zero matching AI mirrors that night and zero promoted `techblog` or `nontech-medium` humans. Lowering Enron memo scores pulls memos out of the top 2% of `docPools`, dropping `prose.LikelyAI` by ~2 to 3 raw points and pushing blogs over `60`. Diagnostic 2 (`+15` memos, `0` new AI: `fresh-blog` `3/536 -> 5/536`, `0.56% -> 0.93%`) and Diagnostic 6 (removing the promoted memos lets `fresh-blog` pass and cuts `nontech-medium` excess FPs from `+15` to `+7` docs, `24/1335 = 1.80%`) isolate this effect.
   - Unpaired multi-mirroring plus `persona`/`continue` boundary shift onto unrepresented casual prose: [`Planner.Next`](../internal/overnight/generate.go#L506-L531) attaches `3` to `6` AI generations (`plain`, `~persona`, `~continue` spanning generators) to existing train-split human sources (`y=0` stays `1`), breaking `1`-to-`1` topic cancellation as [`StylePersona`](../internal/overnight/generate.go#L225-L226) injects first-person narrative prose (`y=1`) into datasets whose human `blog` training set only harvested tech/business-tagged Medium posts ([`sources.go:175-190`](../internal/corpus/sources.go#L175-L190)). Casual non-tech prose (`nontech-medium`) has **zero `y=0` training examples** to push back.
3. Why Night 1 (Qwen) and Night 2 (paid) flipped the same human texts (`[Trusts brief numbers]`): reconstructing precise pool denominators from the brief's percentages yields $N = 536$ for `fresh-blog` (`3 -> 8` docs) and $N = 1,335$ for `nontech-medium` (`17 -> 32/33` docs). Any two refits on the same base corpus maintain human rank correlation $r \approx 0.985$; whenever `blog`/`nontech-medium` rises by ~`+0.20` logit relative to `prose.LikelyAI`, the **same pre-existing borderline human posts sitting at calibrated `55-59` in production** (`#4-#8` of `fresh-blog`; `#18-#33` of `nontech-medium`) tip over `60`.
4. Why `nontech-medium` segment FPR doubled in lockstep (`0.70% -> 1.39% / 1.48%`): [`RetrainCandidate`](../internal/overnight/metrics.go#L284-L286) fits `prose.segment` on **5** pools (`docPools + tech-pool + nontech-medium`) and sets the target segment FPR in [`Thresholds`](../internal/train/train.go#L230-L256) to `Share(segDocRaw, doc.LikelyAI)` over documents with $\ge 2$ segments ($\ge 400$ words, [`engine.go:338`](../internal/engine/engine.go#L338)). Short Enron memos fall out of `segDocRaw` and `nontech-medium` (`1,335` long posts) dominates it; thus whenever `nontech-medium` document FPR rises, `Thresholds` **intentionally loosens** `prose.segment.LikelyAI` to match!

Undermines: Hypothesis 1 as a scalar intercept effect; expecting class weights or subsampling by itself to fix `nontech-medium`; treating segment FPR failures on `nontech-medium` as independent of document FPR failures.

Confidence: High (algebraic proof + live Go simulation on repo training code; empirical decomposition trusts brief numbers).

### F2. Algebraic FNV-1a parity collision puts 100% of `FinalHoldout` inside `calib`, contaminating production cutoffs and skewing candidate calibration

Due to bit-0 linearity of 32-bit FNV-1a, [`FinalHoldout(id)`](../internal/overnight/data.go#L90-L94) is **not** independent of the calibration/check split [`split.Hash(id)%2 == 0`](../internal/train/train.go#L227); **100.00% of `FinalHoldout` files have `split.Hash(id)%2 == 0` (`calib`) with `0.00%` in `check`.** Meanwhile [`Measure`](../internal/overnight/metrics.go#L121) evaluates gate FPR on `calib` + `check` combined versus `check` only.

- In 32-bit FNV-1a, `offset_basis = 2166136261` and `FNV_prime = 16777619` are odd integers. Modulo 2, multiplying by an odd integer in $\mathbb{Z}/2^{32}\mathbb{Z}$ never mixes higher bits into bit 0:
  $$h_{k+1} \bmod 2 \equiv ((h_k \oplus b_k) \times 16777619) \bmod 2 \equiv (h_k \bmod 2) \oplus (b_k \bmod 2)$$
- Prefix `"final-holdout:"` in [`data.go:92`](../internal/overnight/data.go#L92) contains 6 odd-ASCII characters (`i` / `a` / `-` / `o` / `o` / `u`), giving XOR parity `0`. Hence for all `id` values:
  $$\text{FNV1a}_{32}(\text{"final-holdout:"} + \text{Base}(id)) \bmod 2 \equiv \text{split.Hash}(id) \bmod 2$$
- Since `FinalHoldout(id)` checks `Sum32() % 10 == 0` and $10 \mid x \implies 2 \mid x$, **100% of `FinalHoldout` IDs satisfy `split.Hash(id) % 2 == 0`** (verified empirically: `5,023 / 5,023` holdout IDs in `calib`, `0` in `check`). The identical parity bug hits [`corpus.hashN(id, 2*k)`](../internal/corpus/store.go#L168-L172) (`"v:"` has 0 odd bytes), locking `memo`'s 2 prompt templates ([`mirror.go:43-46`](../internal/corpus/mirror.go#L43-L46)) 100% deterministically to `calib` vs `check`.
- Resulting 3 consequences:
  1. Production [`thresholds.json`](../internal/engine/models/thresholds.json#L1-L28) was fit via [`cmd/aidetector/main.go:335`](../cmd/aidetector/main.go#L335) passing nil for `keep`. **100% of the "untouched" `FinalHoldout` sat in production's `calib` set** (making up 20% of `calib`).
  2. Filtering `!FinalHoldout(id)` in [`RetrainCandidate`](../internal/overnight/metrics.go#L269-L287) removes **20% of `calib`** (`943 -> ~754` docs) and **0% of `check`**, skewing `calib:check` to `4:5` (`44.4%` / `55.6%`). Simulating zero-data refits on identical model weights changes integer cutoffs in **79.7%** of trials and rejects the identical model in **23.0%** of trials, explaining **Diagnostic 1** (`0.70% -> 0.77%`, `30 -> 33` segments on `nontech-medium` **`[Trusts brief numbers]`**).
  3. [`Measure`](../internal/overnight/metrics.go#L121) omits `split.Hash(id)%2 == 1` (unlike [`MeasureGenres`](../internal/overnight/measure.go#L138-L140)); thus **44.4% of each calibrated pool's gated FPR is evaluated in-sample** on the identical scores used to select the integer cutoff (`1.93%` mean in-sample FPR vs `2.23%` out-of-sample `check` FPR).

Undermines: Purity of `FinalHoldout` for any production baseline comparison; Diagnostic 1; all calibrated-pool FPR numbers in `Gate`.

Confidence: Certain (algebraic identity + code verification).

### F3. The 43–44 check `Gate` has a ~64%–91% false-rejection rate on neutral retrains, yet leaves rejected toxic data permanently in `corpus/`

[`Gate`](../internal/overnight/metrics.go#L179-L226) combines 43 to 44 one-sided checks via `AND` with `1/N` (`+1` item) FPR tolerances, discrete integer-score `Recall@1%FP` cliffs on pooled 150-doc frozen sets plus silent vacuous-pass bugs when files are absent. Worse, whenever any round is rejected, [`generateAndRetrain`](../internal/overnight/loop.go#L500-L501) leaves all newly generated AI files and promoted human files in `corpus/`, poisoning all subsequent rounds and nights.

1. `+1` item (`1/N`) FWER and segment clustering (`metrics.go:195-208`): spanning 12 document FPR checks and 4 to 5 segment FPR checks, [`oneOf`](../internal/overnight/metrics.go#L171-L176) permits at most **+1 document or +1 segment** regardless of pool size $N$ or baseline count $k_0$. On a neutral retrain ($\mathbb{E}[\Delta\text{FPR}] = 0$) with Skellam paired-flip rate $\lambda = 0.5$ docs/check, 17 FPR checks produce a **64.3% false-rejection rate** (**90.7%** at $\lambda = 1.0$). For segments (`nontech-medium` has ~`4,312` segments spanning `1,335` docs), segments within one post are strongly correlated: if **one** 1,000-word human post shifts such that 2 of the post's 5 segments cross `60`, the candidate fails. Just `0.3` flipping docs/pool yields a **67.0% false-rejection rate**.
2. Integer-quantized `Recall1` cliffs plus correlated frozen sets (`eval.go:143-150`, `metrics.go:97-113`): [`eval.Report.compute`](../internal/eval/eval.go#L143-L150) computes `Threshold1` on rounded integer `res.Score` (`0..100`) and not continuous probability. On `Frozen` PR ($N_h = 150, N_{\text{AI}} = 150$), $\lfloor 0.01 \times 150 \rfloor = 1$; thus `Threshold1` is set by the **2nd-highest human PR**. At logit correlation $r = 0.99$ on a true null, `Threshold1` jumps by `3.0` integer points on average and one frozen PR recall check falsely fails in **16.4%** of trials (`24.6%` on `gpt/pr`). In Night 2's Diagnostic 6 **`[Trusts brief numbers]`**, `frozen Argon PR recall` (`93.3% -> 92.0%`) is `140/150 -> 138/150`, failing `0.01` tolerance by **1 PR** (`138.5` needed). In addition, Opus, Sonnet, Argon plus Gemma reuse the **identical 450 human files and briefs** ([`metrics.go:103`](../internal/overnight/metrics.go#L103)), making `Threshold1` 100% identical among all four and inducing pairwise $\rho = 0.54$ among $\Delta\text{Recall1}$ (`32.0%` combined false failure over the 3 frozen PR checks).
3. Float-scale vs integer-cutoff mismatch (`train.go:187` vs `engine.go:321`): [`FitCutoffs`](../internal/train/train.go#L187) fits cutoffs on rounded integer `Raw`, whereas [`engine.analyze`](../internal/engine/engine.go#L320-L321) scales unrounded `float64` `raw` and rounds the output. Whenever knot spacing exceeds `20` (as in production PR `69 -> 92`, span `23`), `raw = 91.45` has `RawScore = 91 < 92` during calibration despite yielding `Score = 80` (`>= 80`) in `Measure`! Also, for PR (`N_calib ~ 113-143`), `0.5%` allows `0` docs, setting `t.AI = max(calib) + 1` (explaining PR `AI` jumping `92 -> 90` on one sample).
4. Silent vacuous passes (`fixtures.go:310-322`, `metrics.go:201-208`): [`CheckResult.Pass()`](../internal/fixtures/fixtures.go#L310) ignores `r.Missing` (`Pass()` returns `true` when 100% of gitignored fixtures are absent on disk); omitted candidate keys in `PoolDoc`/`PoolSeg` also evaluate `0.0 <= baseline + tol` (`true`).
5. Retained files after rejected rounds (`loop.go:500-501`): rejected rounds write new samples straight to `corpus/*/ai/` and `corpus/*/human/` on disk (with zero rollback), letting one bad batch pollute future nights until cleaned up by hand.

Undermines: Night-2 Diagnostic 6 rejection on `Argon PR recall` (`92.0%` vs `93.3%`); the Night-1 "minus Qwen" pass (`60` samples barely moved weights, holding flip count $\lambda \approx 0$); unattended multi-night safety.

Confidence: High (code + Monte Carlo verification).

### F4. Training-vs-inference preprocessing mismatch and sparse-only L2 bug in `ngram`

Training and scoring clean prose differently.

[`train.Fit`](../internal/train/train.go#L54-L82) trains the 5-fold OOF and production/candidate `prose` n-gram models on **raw file bytes** and never calls [`text.PrepareProse`](../internal/engine/engine.go#L285-L287) to strip non-prose content, whereas inference (`engine.Analyze`, `Thresholds`, `Measure`) strips code blocks, tables, front matter plus Markdown/short label lines *prior to* `ngram.Detect`. Within [`ngram.Train`](../internal/detect/ngram/ngram.go#L160-L162), L2 regularization runs only on active non-zero features per document, giving rare bigrams up to $1{,}000\times$ weaker weight decay.

- In [`train.go:55-81`](../internal/train/train.go#L55-L81), `eval.RunFiltered` calls `engine.Analyze` (applying `PrepareProse`) in order to collect Stage-2 stylometric features `s.Feats`; however, lines `55-60` re-read raw `os.ReadFile(s.Path)` into `docs[i]` and pass `docs` straight to `ngram.Train` and `fm.Logit` (calling only `text.Normalize` at [`ngram.go:59, 97`](../internal/detect/ngram/ngram.go#L59)). Meanwhile, [`corpus.Clean`](../internal/corpus/mirror.go#L221-L227) only calls `stripMarkdown` on `"blog"` and `"essay"`, leaving Markdown headings/lists in `"memo"` (`StylePersona`/`StyleContinue`) and raw code blocks in `"techblog"` (`corpus_tech/blog` has `Kind: "blog"` at [`data.go:39`](../internal/overnight/data.go#L39); on those files `stripMarkdown` strips ` ``` ` markers or leaves unindented code intact) in `ngram.Train`, all of which `PrepareProse` deletes at inference!
- In [`ngram.go:160-162`](../internal/detect/ngram/ngram.go#L160-L162), `for _, j := range r.idx { w[j] -= lr * (g*r.scale + o.L2*w[j]) }` applies `o.L2` (`1e-4`) only when term `j` appears in document `r`, making the effective penalty $\text{L2} \cdot (\text{df}_j / N)$; rare topic bigrams (`df = 3` in `6,000` docs) get $5 \times 10^{-8}$ L2 decay ($1{,}000\times$ weaker than common bigrams), letting unpaired multi-mirrored source topics memorize large weights.

Undermines: OOF calibration alignment of `word_patterns` (weight `1.59` in `prose.json`) and topic-neutrality whenever human sources get multiple AI mirrors.

Confidence: Certain (code inspection).

### F5. Effort experiment (`50 vs 50` unpaired KS) uses a low-power null test backwards and cannot mathematically detect a 100% PR collapse

[`AnalyzeEffort`](../internal/overnight/setup.go#L193-L258) generates paired outputs on the identical 50 sources (`10` per genre spanning 5 genres, `StylePlain` only), discards the pairing, pools `KindProse` (`40`) with `KindPR` (`10`) plus treats failure to reject two-sample KS ($D < 0.2716$ and $|\Delta\bar{s}| \le 3$) as proof that `@low` effort matches default effort.

- At $n_1 = n_2 = 50$, `KSCritical = 1.358 * sqrt(100/2500) = 0.2716` ([`setup.go:245`](../internal/overnight/setup.go#L245)), requiring **at least `14 / 50` (`28%`)** of samples to shift past one score cutoff. Since ~85% of `StylePlain` scores saturate at integers `95..100`, ties reduce actual Type I $\alpha$ from `5.0%` to **`1.22%`**.
- Power collapse: shifting logits by `-0.5` over all 50 samples (more than `2.5x` higher miss rate) is **falsely accepted (`Match = true`) in `96.2%` of trials** (a paired test on the same 50 pairs rejects in **`98.7%`**). Even worse, `pr` is only `10/50` (`20%`) of the pooled slice; thus **even if 100% of PRs fall by 100 points, KS on PRs by itself can never hit `0.2716` ($\max D = 0.20$)**; a `14`-point PR collapse (`72 -> 58`, mean shift `2.8 <= 3.0`) is **falsely accepted in `61.1%` of trials**.
- Train/eval mismatch: `EffortRequests` ([`setup.go:164`](../internal/overnight/setup.go#L164)) never tests `StylePersona` or `StyleContinue`; scoring against `engine.Default()` is also blind to `@low` n-gram artifacts that candidate models subsequently learn when `corpus_frozen` ([`cmd/corpus/overnight.go:151`](../cmd/corpus/overnight.go#L151)) stays at default effort. Enabling `@low` also renames the generator to `<model>@low` ([`corpus/generate.go:35`](../internal/corpus/generate.go#L35)), **wiping out prior bandit counts** and bypassing `Planner` cache checks against `<model>/`!

Undermines: Running Haiku 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 plus Gemini 3.8 Flash at `@low` effort for all paid training data.

Confidence: Certain (power simulation + code inspection).

### F8. Per-genre cutoff measurement (`MeasureGenres`) is NOT invalidated by blog/techblog confusion, yet its NB routing check is distorted

You suspected in the brief that `84.5%` Naive Bayes accuracy (mostly confusing `blog` and `techblog`) might make per-genre cutoff measurements meaningless; however, in [`MeasureGenres`](../internal/overnight/measure.go#L66-L181), the `GenreCutoff` table (`FPROwn`, `RecallOwn`, lines `109-113`) uses **oracle (`true genre`) labels** and never touches Naive Bayes at all! In addition, [`genrePools`](../internal/overnight/measure.go#L26) omits `nontech-medium` (`1,335` docs) altogether.

Undermines: Dismissing oracle per-genre cutoffs on account of `blog`/`techblog` confusion, as well as trusting `detected genre` routing to generalize outside Enron/Seroter.

Confidence: High.

## 3. Direct Answers to Review Questions 1–5

1. Question 1 (Most likely explanation and separating experiment): Neither pure scalar prior shift nor Qwen/continuation noise explains all 7 diagnostics. All 7 diagnostics are jointly explained by **3 additive effects**: (a) the `FinalHoldout` parity bug (`F2`) costing ~2 to 3 segments at baseline (Diag 1), (b) unpaired Enron memo promotions lowering memo scores and pulling the pooled 3-pool cutoff down by ~2 to 3 raw points (Diag 2 & half of Diag 6) plus (c) adding ~1,350 multi-mirrored `plain`/`persona`/`continue` AI samples with uneven per-genre growth and **zero `nontech-medium` human training examples**, raising casual blog logits faster than memo logits (`F1`, explaining why Diag 6 still hits `1.80%` on `nontech-medium` and why Night 1 & Night 2 flip the same borderline human posts). Separating 15-minute experiment: Experiment 1 below (`2x2` refit on existing Night-2 disk data, zero API calls).
2. Question 2 (Soundness of the method):
   - *1:1 mirroring from briefs:* **Sound** when held at `1` human to `1` (or equal $K$) AI mirrors per source; **broken** when the loop piles `3` to `6` AI mirrors onto individual train-split human sources omitting sample weight $K$ on each human source (reintroducing topic leakage via `F4`'s sparse-L2 bug).
   - *Promoting hard-negative humans (`50+`):* **Unsound as implemented.** Promoting only `y=0` outliers from `corpus/memo/pool` (lacking matching `y=1` AI mirrors generated right away for those promoted texts and with zero human pool promotions from casual non-tech blogs or `techblog`) distorts genre score margins and starves pooled cutoffs.
   - *Adding generator-diverse AI text via `<60` miss-rate bandit:* **Partially unsound.** Generator diversity helps recall on unseen models (`plain` mirrors), whereas steering 80% of generation by `Score < 60` pushes positive labels straight into the human stylistic manifold (and into short fragments, truncations plus soft refusals). Weighting bandit arms by **recall shortfall on `StylePlain` per `(genre, generator)`** is far safer than rewarding individual `<60` outliers in `persona`/`continue`.
3. Question 3 (Hold AI:human ratio fixed vs change calibration, plus risks):
   - Never put `nontech-medium` into pooled `docPools` calibration: since `FitCutoffs` targets a **2.0% pooled average**, adding `1,335` `nontech-medium` docs (`2.40%` on Night 2, `1.27%` in prod) paired with `fresh-memo` (`5.57%`) barely nudges the Night-2 cutoff (`72/3032 = 2.37%` vs `40/1697 = 2.36%`), leaving `nontech-medium` around `~2.0%+` (still failing the `1.27%` gate!); in production (`1.27% < 2.0%`) it would **lower** `prose.LikelyAI`, *increasing* false positives in all pools (verified in our live Go simulation).
   - Holding per-genre AI:human weights fixed (via per-sample weights in `ngram.Train` + `calibrate.Fit` and not throwing away data or generating less): necessary in order to stop differential genre intercept drift (retaining 100% of paid variance); however, that is not going to be sufficient by itself when new AI styles (`persona`) overlap unrepresented casual human blogs (`nontech-medium` has 0 training humans).
   - Recommended combination and risks: apply **per-(genre, source) sample weights** such that each human source and the source's $K$ mirrors have equal total positive and negative weight (`1:1` per source and per genre), promote a modest pre-2021 non-tech Medium human slice (paired with mirrors) giving casual prose `y=0` support plus calibrate cutoffs on **continuous logits** using **explicit `input.Genre` when known** or the **worst-case (`max`) per-pool 2% cutoff** (`max_pool Q_0.98(pool)`) when unrouted (risk: raises `prose.LikelyAI` to match `memo`, trading ~2 to 4 points of memo/essay recall for guaranteed $\le 2\%$ FPR on *all* pools with zero reliance on an error-prone text genre classifier).
4. Question 4 (Is the gate measuring the right things with the right noise model?): **Right dimensions, wrong estimator and wrong noise model.** Evaluating FPR partly in-sample (`44.4% calib`), comparing against a nil-`keep` baseline (`F2`), enforcing `+1` doc / `+1` segment over 17 checks lacking multiple-testing or intra-document cluster variance (`64%` to `91%` null FWER) plus computing `Recall1` on quantized integer scores over 150 common frozen humans (`16%` to `25%` one-check null failure) virtually guarantees rejecting genuine improvements as `loop.go:500-501` retains rejected bad data on disk anyway.

## 4. Recommended Experiments (Ordered by Information per Hour, Max 5)

### Experiment 1 (~20 min, \$0 API): 2×2 Orthogonal Refit Separating Prior Shift, Differential Genre Shift, and Feature Drift
Using existing Night-2 files on disk (excluding the promoted memos such that H2 can't confound it), add a `Weight float64` field to [`ngram.Train`](../internal/detect/ngram/ngram.go#L143-L166) and [`calibrate.Fit`](../internal/calibrate/calibrate.go#L150-L175); then run 3 CPU refits against an **uncontaminated baseline** (production weights with cutoffs refit on `noHoldout` after replacing FNV-1a parity with SHA-256 in `FinalHoldout` and `calib`/`check`, evaluated exclusively on out-of-sample `check`):
1. Global prior match: scale all `y=1` weights by one constant matching production's overall AI:human ratio (`1.31:1`).
2. Per-genre plus per-source `1:1` weight match: weight each `(genre, base_id)` group such that total AI weight over all mirrors of `base_id` equals `1.0` (matching the group's `y=0` human source).
3. `StylePlain`-only subset with per-source `1:1` weighting; comparing (1 vs unweighted), (2 vs 1) plus (3 vs 2) separates global prior shift, differential genre/multi-mirror topic drift plus `StylePersona`/`StyleContinue` boundary contamination in one pass.

### Experiment 2 (~20 min, \$0 API): Preprocessing & Sparse-L2 Ablation on Existing Corpus
Apply [`text.PrepareProse`](../internal/engine/engine.go#L286) in [`train.Fit`](../internal/train/train.go#L59) when `kind == detect.KindProse`, fix [`ngram.go:161`](../internal/detect/ngram/ngram.go#L161) to apply dense-vector L2 weight decay on each epoch plus re-run `train.Fit` on (a) pre-Night-1 data and (b) Night-2 data. Comparing the two isolates how much of the Night-2 human score rise came from training `ngram` on stripped-at-inference Markdown/code formatting and under-regularized rare source bigrams.

### Experiment 3 (~15 min, \$0 API): Paired Re-Analysis of Existing `runs/effort/` Outputs + Audit of Night-2 Generated Files
Skipping all API calls, write a 40-line script over the existing `50 vs 50` files in `runs/effort/` computing **paired** continuous logit differences $z_{\text{default}}(i) - z_{\text{low}}(i)$ per genre (Wilcoxon signed-rank + 95% TOST equivalence bounds) and word-count / lexical-diversity shifts. In the same pass, scan Night-2's `~continue` and Gemini files on disk for verbatim 8-word prefix matches against `opening(sm.Text)` and abrupt non-punctuation endings (`MAX_TOKENS` truncations); this reveals right away if `@low` effort degraded PRs or shifted prose logits beneath KS's `0.272` blind spot and quantifies actual human-prefix-repetition / truncation rates sitting in `corpus/`.

### Experiment 4 (~30 min, \$0 API): Continuous-Score & Split-Bootstrap Gate Calibration
Modify [`eval.go:128-151`](../internal/eval/eval.go#L128-L151) to compute `AUROC` and `Recall1` on continuous `Prob` (and not integer `Score`), restrict [`Measure`](../internal/overnight/metrics.go#L121) to `check`-half documents (`SHA256("calib:"+id)%2 == 1`) plus run 500 paired bootstrap resamples (clustered by document ID for segments and by common source ID for `Frozen`) comparing pre-Night-1 CV-fold models. Doing this empirically measures the gate's true null FWER on empirical score distributions and replaces the broken `+1` item / integer-cliff checks with a paired McNemar / document-cluster SE bound calibrated to $\le 5\%$ family-wise false rejection.

### Experiment 5 (~45 min, ~\$0.40 real Haiku/Flash spend): Paired Casual-Blog Human Anchor + Paired Hard-Negative Promotion Test
Take `150` pre-2021 non-tech Medium posts from the **existing train-split portion of a fresh Medium CSV phase** (disjoint from `corpus_nontech/blog/pool` and `fixtures.json`), add those posts to `corpus/blog/human` plus generate **1 matching `StylePlain` mirror** for each (plus **1 matching `StylePlain` mirror** for each of the `16` promoted Enron memos). Refitting with Experiment 1's `1:1` source weights and comparing `nontech-medium` `check`-half FPR and frozen recall tests in isolation if giving casual non-tech prose `y=0` support and pairing hard-negative promotions `1:1` with AI mirrors eliminates the `nontech-medium` and `fresh-blog` FP rise with zero loss in new-model recall.
